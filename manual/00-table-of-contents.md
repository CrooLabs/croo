# The Croo Operator's Manual — table of contents

*Croo Operator's Manual · v1.0 · 2026-10-12*

Style note: each chapter is 2-4 pages, readable on its own, written to be
linked into — by Nova, by the FAQ, by support answers. Every chapter opens
with the one thing it wants you to know, and ends with "Inside Croo? Just
ask Nova." Nothing here duplicates install.md, support.md or the playbooks,
which are their own docs.

## 1. The lay of the land
What Croo is made of, in one sitting: agents, engines, projects, your crew,
and you. Why every agent has a name and one job, and what Nova (your
custodian) does for you. The five words you need before anything else makes
sense.

## 2. The workspace
A tour of croo-web: the fleet at a glance, agent panes side by side, status
states (working, waiting, discussing, resting), Read the room, and how to
arrange it all the way you like. Where to look first when you sit down.

## 3. Your crew
The whole employment story. Hiring (a name, an engine, a job — Nova does
the rest). The identity file: croo scaffolds it, Nova fills it in to
whatever you want, and your words are never overwritten — reading it is how
you understand an agent, editing it is how you improve one. Leave and
return, including cold storage: a crew you don't need this month parks with
its memory intact. Switching an agent to another engine — what carries over
(the identity, updated automatically) and what doesn't (the conversation).
Retiring when the work is done, and restarting fresh.

## 4. Talking to agents
The chat view. Sending text, files and images; one-click answers when an
agent offers choices; macros for the prompts you reuse; pinned drafts for
instructions you're not ready to send; Tidy my draft. Writing briefs agents
act on well.

## 5. Discuss
Putting two agents on one brief until there's a written consensus — to
produce something better than either would alone, or to validate one
agent's plan. Starting a discussion, reading the live commentary, raising
the turn limit mid-discussion when an exchange is earning it, stepping in,
stopping it. Where the consensus lands, and managing the collection: view,
save as a file, archive, delete.

## 6. Mailboxes and handoffs
How work moves between agents: files and messages through mailboxes you can
open and inspect. Reading a handoff, forwarding a message, keeping the paper
trail. Work is passed, not shared — what that buys you.

## 7. Staying in control
Approvals and what agents wait for; Power-Through for the stretches when you
want the work to keep moving; the red button that cuts every standing
approval at once; alerts and the waiting-agent chime. You decide what goes
ahead — this chapter is the mechanics of that sentence.

## 8. Usage and engines
What fills up and what runs out: each agent's conversation, your session and
weekly engine allowances. Reading the rings, compacting an idle agent,
choosing model and effort per agent, pacing a day's work so you use
everything you pay for.

## 9. Croo away from the desk
The phone layout, Telegram (bind a chat to an agent, talk to it directly,
voice in and out), speaking instead of typing, hearing replies read out,
driving mode. What reaches you when you're away, and how to quiet it.
Links "Reach Croo from anywhere" for making your machine reachable first.

## 10. Make it yours
Themes, fonts and pane headers; keyboard shortcuts and the key library;
cues — Croo's reminders — built-in and your own; notes; the Playground; the
local AI and the small jobs it quietly does for you.

## 11. Keeping Croo current
Updates happen inside Croo: the Updates view shows when Croo or an engine
has something new, you prepare it from the workspace, and nothing applies
without your confirmation. Updating engines and why an engine update asks
for an agent restart. Written to what the shipped build does today;
update.md remains the manual/recovery path.

## 12. When something's off
The first move is always the same: describe what you see to Nova, in plain
words. Having her write up a bug report, what to send with your feedback,
and where support.md takes over. Nothing leaves your machine without you.
