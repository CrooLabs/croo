# 2 · The workspace

*Croo Operator's Manual · v1.0 · 2026-10-12*

**The one thing to know: you can see the state of your whole crew
without opening anything.** The workspace is built so a glance answers
"who needs me?" — and everything else is one click deeper.

Croo runs as a local web service on your machine; the workspace is the
page it serves in your browser. (Getting it onto your phone and other
devices is its own short doc: Reach Croo from anywhere.)

## The fleet at a glance

The fleet grid shows one tile per agent, each wearing its state. The
states are worth learning early, because they're the same everywhere in
Croo — grid, agent list, panes:

- **Working** — the agent is on the job; leave it be.
- **Waiting for you** — it needs your word before it can continue. This
  is the state to answer first: a waiting agent is a stopped agent.
- **In a discussion** — it's in a Discuss with another agent.
- **Compacting** — tidying its own conversation; back in a moment.
- **Resting** — idle, ready for work.
- **Stopped** — on leave; its conversation and memory wait for its
  return.

You can sort the agent list by state, so the agents that need you rise
to the top.

## Read the room

Hover over an agent and you get a one-line summary of what it's doing
right now, written by the local AI on your own machine. It's the
fastest answer to "do I need to look at this?" — no pane opened, no
context lost.

## Panes

Open an agent and you get its pane: the conversation, the input box,
and the agent's controls. You can watch up to three agents at once,
split across or down — and that's per browser window: open another
window on another monitor and repeat the process. Any pane pops out
into its own window too.

Two pop-outs earn their keep early. A conversation on a second screen,
for work you're deep in. And the left column — the fleet at a glance —
popped out small in a corner while you're busy with something else in
the foreground: one glance tells you who's working, who's finished and
who needs your attention, without switching away from what you're doing.

How much raw terminal you see is up to you: at full tidy, every agent
reads as the same clean chat whatever its engine, and the raw view is
always one step away.

## Each time you sit down

Make it a habit — the same four places, every time:

1. **The fleet grid** — who's waiting for you? Answer those first.
2. **The usage rings** — the overall resource check: how full each
   agent's conversation is, and how much of your session and weekly
   engine allowances is left. This is how you manage the operation's
   fuel, not just its people. (Chapter 8.)
3. **The mailbox** — what did agents deliver or pass to each other
   while you were away? (Chapter 6.)
4. **Read the room** — a hover over anything that looks unusual.

Ten seconds, and you know the state of the operation. That's the
workspace doing its job.
