# 7 · Staying in control

*Croo Operator's Manual · v1.0.1 · 2026-10-13*

**The one thing to know: nothing goes ahead without your word — and
Croo's job is to make giving that word cheap, not to stop asking.**
This chapter is the mechanics behind the sentence "you decide what
goes ahead."

## Approvals — what agents wait for

An agent pauses before anything that needs your say-so: a command it
wants to run, a file it wants to change, a choice with options. The ask
arrives as one-click buttons in the chat (chapter 4), the agent's tile
turns to **Waiting for you**, and it holds until you answer. That's the
default contract: the agent proposes, you dispose.

The waiting state is loud on purpose — it's the one state you should
never discover late. A soft chime sounds when an agent starts waiting,
and an alert follows if it's still waiting a minute later.

## Power-Through — routine yeses, handled

Some stretches of work are nothing but routine approvals: twenty small
file changes, a chain of harmless commands. For those, switch on
Power-Through: for a time window you choose, Croo answers an agent's
permission prompts for you, so the work keeps moving while you watch
the output instead of pressing yes.

Use it like cruise control: on for the straight road, off the moment
the road gets interesting. The window expires on its own — it's a
stretch, not a policy.

## The red button

One red button in the header cuts off every standing approval at once —
every agent's Power-Through, gone in one press. Be precise about what
it does and doesn't do: it stops the *automatic yeses*, not the agents'
work. Agents keep doing what they were doing; they just go back to
asking you first. It's "hands back on the wheel," not an emergency
stop.

To actually halt an agent, you act on the agent: tell it to stop in
chat, redirect it, or put it on leave from its ⋮ menu (chapter 3).

## Alerts

Agents and tools can raise alerts at four levels of urgency. On your
desk, an alert is spoken aloud or shown as a pop-up — your standing
choice — and one button mutes the lot when you need quiet. (When
you're away from the desk, alerts can reach your phone instead:
chapter 9.)

## The control ladder

Put together, you have a ladder, from lightest touch to firmest:

1. **Answer the ask** — one click, the normal case.
2. **Power-Through** — stop being asked, for a window, where asking is
   noise.
3. **Red button** — every standing approval cut, everyone asks again.
4. **Stop or redirect the agent** — in chat, or ⋮ → Take leave.

Most days you live on rungs 1 and 2. Knowing 3 and 4 are there is what
makes 2 comfortable.
