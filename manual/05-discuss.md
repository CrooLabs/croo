# 5 · Discuss

*Croo Operator's Manual · v1.0 · 2026-10-12*

**The one thing to know: a discussion ends in a written agreement you
can read — and the exchange that produced it is just as valuable.**
Discuss is how you get two minds on one problem without being the
messenger between them.

## What it is

You pick two agents and give them a brief. They take turns on it,
talking directly to each other, until they land at a written
consensus. You watch — or walk away and read the result.

Use it two ways:

- **To produce something better.** Two agents on a new plan land
  somewhere neither would alone. The disagreements along the way are
  where the traps live, and you get to see them.
- **To validate.** One agent has a plan; the other's job is to find
  what's missing, what's riskier than it sounds, what "done" fails to
  pin down. If the plan survives, you proceed with confidence.

When *not* to use it: routine handoffs. "Pass the build to Elsa for
review" is a mailbox message (chapter 6), not a discussion. Discuss
earns its turns on questions with real room to disagree.

## Running one

The easiest way: bring both agents up on screen, then ⋮ → Discuss. The
discussion starts from there — you give the brief to the agent you
started it from, the agent of origin. That agent carries the exchange
and owns the consensus at the end; the handshake glyph next to its
name is where you'll find it. (Or just tell Nova what you want
discussed and by whom.)

While it runs:

- **The banner** shows the turn count and the turn limit. If the
  exchange is earning it, raise the limit mid-discussion and let it
  run; a good argument is cheap compared to a bad build.
- **Live commentary** narrates the exchange in plain language as it
  happens — generated on your own machine by the local AI — so you can
  follow the shape of the debate without reading every word of it.
- **You can talk to either agent while they discuss** — ask a side
  question, add a fact they're missing — as you follow the commentary
  in real time. And you can step in or stop the discussion at any
  time. It's your meeting; the agents just talk faster than most.

## The consensus

When they agree, the agreement is written down — that's the product of
the whole exercise. It lives with the agent of origin: the handshake
glyph next to its name takes you there. Build from the consensus, not from memory: it's
what the reviewer later checks the work against, and the difference
between "we agreed" and "I think we agreed."

Every consensus is kept. View it, save it as a file, archive it when
it's served its purpose, or delete it. The good ones are worth saving
into the project — a consensus is often the best design document you
didn't have to write.

## Habits that pay

- **Discuss before building anything non-trivial.** The cheapest time
  to find a flaw is before the first file is written.
- **Brief the discussion like work, not like a debate.** Give both
  agents the same facts you'd give one: what you want, what "done"
  means, what you're afraid of.
- **Read the commentary even when you skip the exchange.** Two minutes
  tells you whether the consensus was hard-won or a rubber stamp — and
  those deserve different levels of trust.
